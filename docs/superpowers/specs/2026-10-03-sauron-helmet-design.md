# Sauron foam helmet — provisional paper-template design

Status: **paper-fit design study**, not a fitted or foam-ready fabrication pattern. Revision 2, 2026-10-03. The user requested a more movie-like middle ground, explicit spike patterns, and a provisional size that can be changed later. No helmet has been fabricated or wear-tested.

## Purpose and scope

Design original full-size paper cutting drawings for a recognizable Sauron helmet for the same nine-year-old as the mace. Use layered EVA-style face plates and six faceted crown spires, inspired by production-helmet front, side, and rear photographs. Keep an open back/top, a soft adjustable support, generous sight openings, and flexible rounded points. Omit intricate engraving, a closed dome, rigid spike cores, eye mesh, chin straps, and printing hardware.

The deliverable in this revision is **the physical design drawing set for paper fitting and user review**, not a software subsystem or a parametric CAD generator. SVGs are the editable drawing masters. PDF conversion uses existing installed tools; no new product dependencies or permanent generator are needed. No mace source, export contracts, or generated mace files should change.

## Reference and evidence boundaries

Primary reference is the Propstore production helmet, not a tracing of the Woodland Elf PDF. Five photographs were visually inspected. The movie-like features to preserve are the long split lower face, narrow center ridge, angular eye/brow frame, layered swept cheek/temple plates, and faceted spires. The six principal spires are interpreted as a tall side pair, shorter forward pair, central front spire, and central rear spire. Small edge points are decorative, not additional principal spires.

## Provisional size and scaling

| Item | Drawing assumption, not measured fit |
|---|---|
| Headband loop | 540 mm after joining and rear adjustment overlap |
| Eye center spacing | 60 mm nominal; actual sight openings must be located on the wearer |
| Brow frame development | 170 mm wide; shallow wrapping changes its apparent front width |
| Central face ridge | 42 × 210 mm flat outline; narrow through the eye region |
| Main cheek plate | 70 × 196 mm flat outline, cut twice mirrored |
| Tall side spires | 50 × 205 mm, including a 25 mm root; cut twice mirrored |
| Short forward spires | 44 × 155 mm, including a 25 mm root; cut twice mirrored |
| Central front spire | 42 × 185 mm, including a 25 mm root; cut once |
| Central rear spire | 42 × 175 mm, including a 25 mm root; cut once |

A nominal 540 mm circumference is a convenient provisional size, **not an age-based recommendation**. The mask's eye openings, cheek clearance, band tension, spire length/stiffness, and support balance remain to be fitted independently.

For an initial uniform paper trial, print all cutting sheets at the same percentage. If a measured headband path is H mm, H/540 × 100% is only a starting scale, not proof of facial fit. A 100 mm scale ruler then prints at 100 × that factor mm; do not demand 100 mm after deliberately scaling. At the supplied 100% print setting, it must measure 100 mm. Record changes on the paper masters. Changing eye spacing requires moving/enlarging openings, not blindly applying circumference scale. For enlargement use Poster/Tile Scale printing or resize the SVG canvas and artwork together; ordinary scaling on a fixed sheet may clip parts near the margins. Keep paper-fitting mandatory before foam.

## Drawing set

Create four cutting-sheet SVG masters under `patterns/helmet/`, each in millimeters on a Letter-sized canvas. All pieces fit wholly on their sheet and inside the A4 printable area; **no piece spans pages**, so unnecessary page tiling/taping is avoided. Make five-page Letter and A4 PDF packs containing the four cutting sheets plus an assembly key. In the A4 pack, change paper dimensions without scaling artwork. Include an assembled preview PNG for quick visual review.

One template is provided per distinct shape. For mirrored pairs, trace onto spare paper and flip the template for the second piece; print selected pages again if preferred. Do not turn a drawn front silhouette into a cutting pattern: the flat components and assembled illustration are deliberately separate.

Solid black lines are cuts, including eye and breathing openings. Dashed blue lines are placement/bond-zone references, not cuts. Dash-dot green lines indicate a shallow paper forming fold, not a foam knife cut. Gray labeled diamonds are matching placement anchors; alignment remains adjustable during fitting. Use line styles and labels as well as colors for grayscale printing. Show ID, quantity, mirroring, and eventual foam thickness on every shape's sheet. Every PDF page carries **PAPER FIT ONLY**, the nominal size, print instruction, and a 100 mm physical scale ruler.

## Paper support and component assembly

Use light cardstock/paper, a ruler, scissors, and tape. Adult supervision; cover sharp paper edges, and do not put pins/staples near the wearer.

1. Cut six copies of H, a 25 × 165 mm paper strip. Four strips make the band: join three seams with 20 mm overlap, giving 600 mm developed length; a separate 60 mm adjustable rear overlap gives a 540 mm loop. Two further strips joined with 20 mm overlap give a 310 mm over-head strap. Its provisional 20 mm attachment at each end leaves a 270 mm over-head span. Adjust on the actual child rather than forcing these dimensions. Paper tape closures should open easily.
2. Fit band and side-to-side over-head strap loosely. The child must be able to lift them off and reach the rear overlap. This strap locates band height; it does not establish stability against forward mask weight.
3. Curve A gently around the face and place B over its center using the A/B anchor. B is narrow at eye level to avoid covering A's eye openings, with a split lower outline and a separate breathing slot. Tape B's upper A/B area only; leave its lower portion free. The short forming guide below eye level allows a gentle forward angle away from the nose/mouth. Fold K to establish an adjustable forehead stand-off instead of laying the facade directly against the forehead. These paper adjustments do not guarantee clearance: fit the actual child, and recheck tipping when the gap changes. Enlarge or shorten anything that obstructs breathing or sight.
4. Attach C to each side at the A/C anchor; wrap it toward the side, rather than treating its entire flat width as added frontal width. Attach D behind the cheek/frame at the A/D anchor. Where plates overlap, temporarily tape a small contact area on the outside. These are adjustable lap placements, not precision edge-to-edge seams. Keep the back open.
5. Fold K along its center guide: center its face half behind A in the marked forehead zone, and tape its band half to the front band, bending as needed to establish clearance. Rotate each L a quarter-turn so a 25 × 20 mm half matches A's 20 × 25 mm blue side box; tape that half directly behind A and fold the other toward the band at the temple. Attach L before D, and position D behind the sides without covering L's fold or free band half; D is decoration, not the only support attachment. Use broad outside tape contact areas. Forehead **and** temple attachment are required; do not hang the face from two temple tabs alone. Add small soft pads only if necessary and keep sight openings clear.
6. Add E and G only after basic fitting. Place C2 using its two C/C2 anchors, lining up the corresponding diamonds on C. These are decorative overlay pieces; they must not cover the eyes, breathing slot, or removal path. Overlays can be omitted if too bulky.
7. Gently form S1–S4 along their center guides into shallow open-backed V sections; a roughly 120° interior angle is an aesthetic paper starting point, not a calibrated requirement. Roots are 25 mm long. Fold/curve the root to suit its crown position, then tape it to the support with the root allowance overlapping the band. **Do not add the 25 mm root again** to the cut outline. The six positions are S1 front center, S4 left/right forward, S3 left/right sides, S2 rear center. Points can lean outward slightly, but check overhead clearance and balance.
8. Add points incrementally, testing whether the paper facade tips or slips toward the eyes. All principal spires have visibly blunted ends, not mathematical needle tips. Paper may still have sharp corners on crests, cheeks, or other edges; tape or round them further for fitting. If unstable, stop and revise the support rather than tightening painfully or adding a chin strap.

Assembly illustrations communicate layers and approximate placement, **not verified 3D joint geometry**. The rear center root must mount beside/above the reachable rear band overlap, not tape across it; the release/removal path stays clear. No closed rear shell is required.

## Eventual EVA construction, after fitting

Tentative materials: 4 mm EVA for A–D and spires, 2 mm for E/G/C2, soft 4–6 mm EVA for support patches, approximately 25 mm soft webbing for the final support. Those are starting choices, not stiffness/comfort specifications. Thin 4 mm spires may fold: shorten/widen them before considering heavier foam. No wire, rods, dowels, resin, or hardened coating.

The paper H strips are **support fitting aids**, not instructions to make the final wearable band out of cardstock or long stiff EVA strips. Transfer the accepted support lengths to adjustable webbing, sew webbing joints, and bond foam through broad manufacturer-compatible scrap-tested patches. Rear hook-and-loop must be reachable without catching hair. No chin/neck strap.

Paper crease lines are forming guides. Foam can be heat-shaped off the child to a shallow ridge; do not cut through the center line. Do not treat paper folds as a tested EVA development. Round all exposed nose, cheek, and spire edges, and test the first cured foam brow/support before adding cheeks and points. Repeat fitting after every substantial addition. No hot glue or heat shaping on the wearer. An adult handles cutting, heat, solvent adhesives, and painting with manufacturer ventilation/PPE/fire precautions; keep the child away from the work area.

Finish only after physical fitting passes. Use flexible compatible coatings, black/dark gunmetal and restrained silver edge highlights, not a hard resin shell. Fully cure/ventilate adhesives and coatings to their instructions before wear; odor absence is not a cure test.

## Acceptance and verification

Document checks must verify:

- All four masters have the intended physical millimeter sizes and quantity labels, including all six principal spires via the cut list.
- Black cutting geometry is wholly within each page, with no component clipped or split, and IDs/placement marks remain legible.
- Final Letter and A4 PDF pages have the correct paper dimensions and reproduce actual cutting-path segments and placement/fold references at the same physical scale. Checking only the separate ruler is insufficient.
- Every page has its 100 mm ruler and provisional warning. Deliberately distort a copied cutting path while leaving the ruler unchanged; the physical-geometry comparison must fail before trusting it.
- Front illustration and crown-position diagram agree with the cut IDs/counts, with illustration explicitly labeled not to cut. Render and inspect pages visually; quantitative checks do not prove a usable drawing.

Physical checks remain unverified until the child tries the paper and then the foam version. Begin stationary, then supervised walking on a clear flat floor; no running/stair trial. Stop immediately for discomfort, dizziness, breathing difficulty, blocked vision, or instability. Check floor/peripheral sight, speech/breathing, head turns/downward look, glasses clearance if relevant, forehead/nose/ear pressure, independent removal, crown/body/door clearance, and alignment. The whole costume can change shoulder clearance. Later foam wear trials must separately check cured joint retention, finished weight/balance, flex/softness, and heat buildup; a brief indoor trial does not establish long-event comfort. This is not protective headgear or a safety certification.
